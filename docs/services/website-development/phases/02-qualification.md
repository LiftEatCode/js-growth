# Website Development Phase 2: Qualification

> **Part of the JS Solutions Website Development Playbook**

---

# Purpose

The Qualification phase determines whether a prospective client and project are a good fit for JS Solutions.

Not every project should be accepted.

Successful projects begin with the right expectations, the right budget, the right timeline, and a strong partnership.

The goal is to ensure that both JS Solutions and the client are positioned for success before moving into planning and execution.

---

# Objectives

By the end of this phase, we should understand:

- Whether the project aligns with our expertise.
- Whether the client's expectations are realistic.
- Whether the project budget supports the desired outcome.
- Whether the timeline is achievable.
- Whether there are any major risks or blockers.
- Whether we are the best partner for the project.

---

# Qualification Philosophy

Our goal is not to close every sale.

Our goal is to build long-term relationships with clients we can genuinely help.

Sometimes the best decision is to decline a project or recommend a different solution.

Protecting the client's interests ultimately protects our reputation.

---

# Ideal Client Profile

The best clients typically:

- Value quality over the lowest price.
- View technology as an investment.
- Communicate openly and honestly.
- Are committed to growing their business.
- Respect timelines and collaboration.
- Trust professional recommendations.
- Are willing to invest in long-term success.

---

# Red Flags

Projects should receive additional review if any of the following are present:

- Unrealistic deadlines.
- Unrealistic budget.
- Constantly changing requirements.
- Poor communication.
- No clear decision maker.
- Previous disputes with multiple agencies.
- Requests for unethical SEO or marketing practices.
- Requests to copy another company's website.
- Lack of ownership over domains or hosting.
- Refusal to participate in the discovery process.

One red flag does not automatically disqualify a project, but multiple red flags should be discussed before moving forward.

---

# Budget Evaluation

Determine:

- Has the client established a budget?
- Does the budget align with the project scope?
- Does the client understand the long-term cost of maintaining a website?
- Are additional services expected?

If budget limitations exist, consider reducing project scope rather than reducing quality.

---

# Timeline Evaluation

Questions to ask:

- Is there a required launch date?
- Why is that date important?
- Is the timeline realistic?
- What dependencies exist?
- What approvals will be required?

Never promise a timeline before understanding the full project scope.

---

# Technical Fit

Evaluate whether JS Solutions has the experience, resources, and capacity required.

Consider:

- Required integrations
- Custom functionality
- Third-party software
- Security requirements
- Compliance requirements
- Performance expectations

If outside expertise is required, identify it before accepting the project.

---

# Decision Makers

Identify:

Primary Decision Maker

Technical Contact

Marketing Contact

Billing Contact

Project Stakeholders

Understanding who approves decisions early helps avoid unnecessary delays later.

---

# Content Readiness

Determine whether the client already has:

- Logo
- Brand guidelines
- Photography
- Videos
- Service descriptions
- Employee biographies
- Testimonials
- Case studies
- Legal policies
- Frequently asked questions

Missing content should be documented as a project dependency.

---

# Success Criteria Review

Confirm that the client's expectations are measurable.

Examples include:

- Increase qualified leads.
- Improve conversion rate.
- Improve search visibility.
- Improve page speed.
- Modernize branding.
- Simplify content management.

Avoid vague goals such as "make it look better" without defining measurable outcomes.

---

# Internal Risk Assessment

Before proceeding, document:

## Low Risk

Routine website project with clear goals and expectations.

## Medium Risk

Project includes moderate complexity, integrations, or scheduling concerns.

## High Risk

Project contains significant uncertainty, unrealistic expectations, or technical challenges requiring additional planning.

---

# Qualification Checklist

Before moving forward, confirm:

- [ ] Discovery completed.
- [ ] Business goals understood.
- [ ] Budget discussed.
- [ ] Timeline reviewed.
- [ ] Decision makers identified.
- [ ] Technical feasibility confirmed.
- [ ] Major risks documented.
- [ ] Client expectations aligned.
- [ ] Required content identified.
- [ ] Project appears beneficial for both parties.

---

# Deliverables

At the completion of Qualification, the following should exist:

- Discovery notes
- Client summary
- Risk assessment
- Initial project scope
- Budget confirmation
- Timeline estimate
- Recommendation to proceed or decline

---

# Exit Criteria

Qualification is complete when:

- Both parties understand the project.
- Expectations are aligned.
- Risks have been documented.
- The project is technically feasible.
- Budget and timeline are acceptable.
- JS Solutions is confident in delivering a successful outcome.

---

# Key Principle

The right clients lead to the best projects.

Choosing projects carefully allows JS Solutions to maintain quality, build trust, and create long-term partnerships instead of short-term transactions.
