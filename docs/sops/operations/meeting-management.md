# Standard Operating Procedure: Meeting Management

> **Part of the JS Solutions Standard Operating Procedures**

---

# Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines how meetings are planned, conducted, documented, and followed up at JS Solutions.

The objective is to ensure meetings are productive, purposeful, and result in clear decisions, assigned action items, and documented outcomes.

Meetings should create value—not interrupt productive work.

---

# Scope

This SOP applies to:

- Discovery meetings
- Client meetings
- Proposal reviews
- Project kickoff meetings
- Internal planning sessions
- Strategy meetings
- Vendor meetings

---

# JS Solutions Standards

Every meeting should:

- Have a clear purpose
- Include an agenda
- Start and end on time
- Produce documented outcomes
- Assign action items
- Minimize unnecessary attendees
- Respect everyone's time

If a meeting can be replaced by documentation or a short message, it should be.

---

## Meeting Organizer

- Scheduling the meeting

- Following up on action items

# Procedure

## Step 1 — Define the Objective

Before scheduling the meeting, answer:

"What decision or outcome should this meeting produce?"

If the answer is unclear, reconsider whether a meeting is necessary.

---

## Step 2 — Prepare an Agenda

Include:

- Meeting objective
- Discussion topics
- Decisions required
- Time allocation
- Expected outcomes

Distribute the agenda before the meeting whenever practical.

---

## Step 3 — Prepare Materials

Gather:

- Project documentation
- Proposals
- Reports
- Analytics
- Design mockups
- Relevant notes

Ensure participants have the information needed for productive discussion.

---

## Step 4 — Conduct the Meeting

During the meeting:

- Stay on agenda
- Encourage participation
- Capture important decisions
- Identify risks
- Assign action items

Avoid unrelated discussions.

---

## Step 5 — Document Outcomes

Record:

- Key decisions
- Questions raised
- Risks identified
- Action items
- Responsible owner
- Due dates

Meeting notes become part of the project documentation.

---

## Step 6 — Communicate Follow-Up

After the meeting:

Send a summary including:

- Decisions made
- Assigned tasks
- Deadlines
- Next meeting (if applicable)

Ensure everyone understands expectations.

---

## Step 7 — Track Action Items

Review action items regularly.

Confirm:

- Completion
- Updated status
- Blockers
- Revised deadlines

Close completed items promptly.

---

# Common Issues

| Issue | Resolution |
|--------|------------|
| No agenda | Prepare one before scheduling |
| Meeting runs long | Assign a timekeeper and stay on topic |
| No decisions made | Clarify the objective before the meeting |
| Action items forgotten | Document and assign owners before ending |
| Too many attendees | Invite only essential participants |

---

# Success Criteria

Meeting management is successful when:

- Meetings have a clear purpose
- Decisions are documented
- Action items are assigned
- Follow-up is completed
- Projects move forward efficiently
